🚀 The Tyranny of O(N²)

When an algorithm runs in quadratic time, doubling the input size $N \to 2N$ multiplies the running time by 4. Multiplying $N$ by 10 increases the time by 100! No hardware upgrade or supercomputer can compensate for quadratic explosion as data scales to millions of records.

⚡ The Power of O(N log N)

Divide-and-conquer breaks the problem into $O(\log N)$ levels of linear work $\Theta(N)$. Doubling $N \to 2N$ only adds one additional level of work, increasing time by a factor of roughly $2 \times (1 + 1/\log N) \approx 2.1$. This scalability enables sorting millions of items in a fraction of a second.

🎯 When Does Insertion Sort Win?

For tiny arrays ($N < 10$) or arrays that are nearly sorted ($c \cdot N$ inversions), Insertion Sort runs in linear time $O(N)$ with zero recursion stack overhead and zero heap allocations. Modern production algorithms (like Java's DualPivotQuicksort and Python's Timsort) switch to Insertion Sort for small partitions!
